Archer vs Assassin — AI Battle

A Unity experiment where AI units learn to fight each other

Built with Claude. I came up with the idea and decided what to build, but Claude — an AI coding assistant — wrote and debugged most of the C# code with me.

This is the biggest project I've attempted. It's a Unity simulation where 100 AI units — 50 archers and 50 assassins, split into two teams — fight in a 120×80 arena. Each unit runs a small neural network that decides how to move, aim, and attack. Every generation, the best archer and the best assassin have their "brain" copied to the rest of their class and slightly mutated, so over many generations the units gradually get better.

What's actually in it

  • A hand-written neural network (48 inputs → 48 hidden → 7 outputs) in C#
  • A genetic algorithm that clones and mutates the best units each generation
  • A vision system where teammates share what they see, and cover blocks sight
  • An ammo system for archers, with supply points on the map
  • Save/load, so training can be paused and resumed

What I wanted vs. what actually happened

The tactics you see — staying near an ally, going for ammo when low, using cover — are all rules I gave the units by hand. What I actually hoped for was that the AI would learn to discover new tactics on its own, beyond what I wrote. It didn't get there: the units mostly just follow the rules I gave them, and the neural network hasn't learned to come up with anything genuinely new. That's the part I still want to get working.
