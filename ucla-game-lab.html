🃏 Lethal Spire: 100 Ways to Die

Role: Team Leader & Game Designer (UCLA Summer School Project)

Project Overview

A tabletop card game developed during the UCLA summer school program. I led a 4-person team to design and produce the game from concept to a finished physical product.

My Role & Responsibilities

As the Team Leader and Game Designer, I was responsible for the project's vision and execution:

  • Game Design: Conceptualized and designed the core game mechanics, wrote the rulebook, and balanced gameplay.
  • Team Leadership: Managed a team of four, coordinating tasks and ensuring the project was completed on schedule.

Team Coordination

  • Art Team (2 members): Directed the visual style, card illustration, and overall aesthetic.
  • Research & Development (1 member): Collaborated on building the game's world and lore by researching relevant materials and brainstorming creative ideas.

🎨 Art Style

The game features a unique Gothic dark-fairytale aesthetic. The visual design uses a deep blue color palette to create a mysterious atmosphere, populated by anthropomorphic characters like a skeleton wearing an earring. The art is framed with vintage patterns and stylized fonts like "Night Dancer," creating an elegant yet eerie visual tension that immerses players in the game's world.

🎲 Gameplay Mechanics

Flipping the traditional RPG script, this game puts players in the shoes of the main villain boss rather than the hero. Instead of going on an adventure, your goal is to ruthlessly defend your lair. The hero advances automatically each turn, and you must use dice rolls to trigger deadly traps and manipulate scene effects. It is a strategic battle of attrition where you must defeat the hero before they reach the final destination.

3D Game Project — Zombie Survival Prototype

In July 2025, I built a 3D survival game prototype in Unity during the UCLA Game Lab Summer Institute — entirely on my own. Set in a zombie world, the goal is to collect four experience orbs to complete the level.

The game opens on a road lined with abandoned cars, leading toward a distant town with ruined houses on both sides. From the very start, the player can look up and see the mountain peak where the final orb floats — a deliberate visual hint of the goal ahead. At its core, this project was an exercise in guided level design: steering the player forward through the environment without relying on text instructions.

The first orb sits right at the start of the road, teaching the player what to look for. The next two are tucked into the narrow gaps between the ruined houses on the left, rewarding a careful search. An invisible wall — paired with a small on-screen hint — blocks players who try to climb the mountain directly, gently funneling them along the intended path. Finally, a glowing portal on the right side of the road teleports the player to the peak, where the last orb floats in mid-air and must be collected by leaping from the peak and falling through it.

I made some of the assets myself and used free resources for others. Built in a short time, this prototype was a focused way to practice a range of new skills:

  • Visuals & Environment: a custom sky shader, terrain sculpting, and scene composition.
  • Core Mechanics: a working portal teleportation system.
  • Technical Effects: custom particle systems.
  • Audio: realistic footstep sounds and ambient zombie noises, implemented through Unity's native audio system.
